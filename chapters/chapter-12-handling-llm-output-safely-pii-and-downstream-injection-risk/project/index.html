Chapter 12 Project ~75 min, hands-on

Project: Find and Fix TicketSense's Output-Handling Gaps

A find-and-fix defense lab, matching Chapters 9-10's pattern — not a findings-report deliverable like Chapter 11's. See README.md for why this chapter's own subject (building output-side controls) fits a find-and-fix lab better than another findings report.

The target starter.py ships a complete, runnable, intentionally vulnerable slice of TicketSense, Fenwick Customer Experience's support platform — the same fictional system from lesson.html's hook, reproducing both of its incidents mechanically (PII leakage in a generated summary, an unescaped HTML payload in a generated reply) plus a third failure this chapter also names (an unvalidated auto-fetched related-case link).
What you do Run the target as-is to see all three naive vulnerabilities reproduce, then implement three real defenses — redact_pii(), html_escape_output(), and is_allowed_case_link() — and confirm, by re-running the target, that all three failures are closed with no regression on legitimate ticket behavior. Grade your finished implementation against RUBRIC.md's five criteria.
starter.py solution.py (reference) RUBRIC.md README.md (full brief)

1 Run the naive target

python3 starter.py demonstrates all three vulnerabilities against the deterministic target with zero edits needed.

2 Implement the PII redaction defense

Fill in redact_pii() to close the summary-leakage failure, and understand its honest limit.

3 Implement the rendered-output injection defense

Fill in html_escape_output() and confirm it's applied before interpolation, not after.

4 Implement the downstream-API injection defense

Fill in is_allowed_case_link(), resistant to look-alike-subdomain tricks.

5 Confirm and self-grade

Re-run starter.py to confirm all three failures are closed with no regression, then grade against RUBRIC.md (target: 18-20/20).

See README.md for the full brief and RUBRIC.md for the complete grading rubric.
