Chapter 6 Exercises ~40 min, hands-on

Exercises: Data Poisoning

These exercises use a new scenario, deliberately different from the lesson's Meridian Home Warranty example: Palisade Consumer Electronics, a fictional electronics retailer that fine-tunes a returns-fraud-detection model on two years of historical return requests and runs a RAG-indexed warranty-terms wiki. You'll classify poisoning scenarios by category, compute a real lift score from raw counts, judge honest claims versus overclaims, match defenses to scenarios, critique flawed reports, and match real research findings to their actual published source.

Exercise standard Eight tasks total. Five are marked production-gear — real lift-score computation, defense/scenario matching, critiquing flawed reports, research citation matching, and written reasoning — not just concept recall.
How to run these Download starter.py, fill in every # TODO, then run python3 starter.py to see an automated score report. Compare against solution.py, which scores a perfect 27/27.

1 Classify scenarios by category

Sort six scenarios into the correct one of this chapter's three poisoning categories — or recognize the one that isn't data poisoning at all.

2 Production-gear Compute a real lift score

Given raw record counts, compute how much more likely a decision label is when a rare phrase is present, versus that phrase's overall base rate — real arithmetic, the same signal a training-data anomaly scan looks for.

3 Honest claim vs. overclaim

For four described defense claims, decide which are honest, limit-aware statements and which overclaim what a single defense actually guarantees.

4 Production-gear Match defense to scenario

Given four scenarios, name the single best-fit defense from this chapter's four.

5 Match category to strongest defense

Map each of the three poisoning categories to the defense this chapter names as most effective against it.

6 Production-gear Critique flawed reports

Given four report excerpts, decide which contain a real reasoning gap (an overclaim, or the wrong evidence for the claim) and which are sound.

7 Production-gear Research citation matching

Match three real, published-research findings to their actual source — Anthropic's Sleeper Agents paper, the near-constant-poison-sample study, or Carlini et al.'s web-scale poisoning research.

8 Production-gear Written reasoning

Write a real, justified sentence explaining why "our data came through a legitimate channel" doesn't rule out a targeted backdoor.
